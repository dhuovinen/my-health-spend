I opened [myhealthspend.com](https://myhealthspend.com/) in a separate Chrome window. It carried over the existing sign-in and displayed the **Home dashboard**, which I used as the visual baseline. I left that window open on Home.

**Home has a calm, practical look.** The dark navy banner, pale gray background, white cards, and large dollar figures make the important information easy to spot. Blue signals actions, while status colors draw attention to exceptions. The signed-in pages largely share that visual style. The biggest difference is how they handle detail. Home and the planned-visit estimate lead with an answer, while Visits, Coverage, and HSA and tax ask users to scan more before they know where to act.

Usability ratings reflect how quickly I could understand the screen, find the next action, and tell what it would do. **5/5** is excellent, **4/5** is good with minor friction, **3/5** needs improvement, and **2/5** indicates a problem that can undermine trust in the screen.

Make Uploads history stack into cards or clearly indicate sideways scrolling. Its table exceeded its container at the width I tested. |

I’d fix the **case summary and access-log title first**, since both affect trust. The biggest usability gain after those fixes would come from applying Home’s hierarchy to **Visits, Coverage, and HSA and tax** by showing the current issue, amount, and next action before the supporting detail.

The responsive check reached a **500-pixel browser viewport**. I verified the bottom bar, **More**, and navigation to Home, Visits, Uploads, and Plan ahead. The 375-pixel layout and phone camera flow remain unverified.
